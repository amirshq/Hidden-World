The Project

What is Hidden World, and why?

What it is

A collaborative project to find, photograph, and responsibly document the hidden parts of nature at night — subjects that reveal unique characteristics under UV-A. The goal: document this unseen world artistically, with every subject kept in its natural environment.

Why the idea was formed

It grew out of our background in astrophotography — capturing faint nebulae and hidden structures of the Milky Way the eye can’t see.

“What hidden beauties exist in the nature around us at night that we simply cannot see?”

The founding question

The Subjects

Four groups of natural subjects

Animals

Nocturnal creatures: scorpions, spiders, and the wider insect family.

Plants & lichens

Some reveal striking fluorescent patterns and colors under UV.

Fungi

Mushrooms and other fungal forms found out in nature.

Minerals & crystals

Beautiful fluorescence or phosphorescence; abundant across Iran.

In the wild, not the studio. Every subject is photographed in its natural habitat.

Method & Science

How we work — and what makes it glow

How we do it

Our main tool is UV-A light at 365 nm and 395 nm. We search, observe each subject’s reaction, photograph it, and record the details.

We log for each discovery

Location & date · Light source & wavelength · Camera, lens & EXIF · Type of light response

What is fluorescence?

A material absorbs ultraviolet energy and almost instantly re-emits it as visible light — usually in a different color.

In the dark, a scorpion looks ordinary. Under UV-A, its body glows a beautiful blue-green.
